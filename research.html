Curvature in Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing

Investigating how robotic toolpath curvature influences metal deposition and bead geometry.

01 — THE QUESTION

How does the shape of a robot's path change what it produces?

During my 2026 summer research at Wesleyan University, I investigated how curvature in robotic wire arc additive manufacturing affects the geometry of deposited metal.

The project explored the relationship between digital toolpaths and physical results, examining how changes in curvature and process parameters influence the final bead.

02 — THE EXPERIMENT

From digital path to physical object.

I designed and tested curved robotic deposition paths across multiple combinations of curvature, wire feed rate, and travel speed.

The resulting deposits were measured and analyzed to understand how toolpath geometry affected bead width and height.

03 — THE PROCESS

01

Designed

Developed curved robotic toolpaths with controlled geometric and process parameters.

02

Fabricated

Produced curved metal deposits using a robotic MIG welding system.

03

Measured

Collected physical measurements from the deposited beads at multiple locations.

04

Analyzed

Used MATLAB and mathematical modeling to investigate relationships between curvature and bead geometry.

04 — THE WORK

05 — WHAT I FOUND

Curvature matters.

The study showed that toolpath curvature can substantially influence deposited bead geometry, particularly as curvature becomes small relative to the characteristic bead width.

Detailed quantitative results are currently being prepared for publication and are not included here.

07 — SHARING THE RESEARCH

From the lab to the stage.
