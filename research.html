Part I · Wesleyan University · 2026

Curves in 3D Printing Steel

Predictive modeling of how robotic toolpath curvature influences metal deposition and bead geometry in Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing (WAAM).

Lab
Chang-Davidson Lab, Wesleyan
Role
Undergraduate researcher · June 2026–present
Process
WAAM · ABB robot + Fronius GMAW welder
Status
First-author manuscript under advisor review

01 — The question

How does the shape of a robot's path change what it produces?

I lead this study at Wesleyan University's Chang-Davidson Lab, working one-on-one with PI Elizabeth Chang-Davidson, Ph.D. The lab focuses on steel additive manufacturing with a robotic MIG (GMAW) welding setup.

The project looks at how curved toolpaths and process parameters (wire feed rate, travel speed and toolpath radius) shape the steel bead that gets deposited: a critical manufacturing consideration when translating design into physical objects.

02 — The experiment

Programming the path and welding the samples.

I program an ABB industrial robot paired with a Fronius GMAW welder in RobotStudio to deposit steel beads along curved toolpaths, across a matrix of toolpath radius, wire feed rate and travel speed.

I collected and analyzed more than 1,000 bead width measurements in MATLAB. To check them, I section samples on a waterjet cutter, mount them in acrylic, then grind, polish and etch them for optical microscopy, using the cross-sections to validate the caliper measurements and spot porosity defects.

04 — What I found

Curvature matters.

Toolpath curvature substantially changes deposited bead geometry, especially as the curve gets tight relative to the bead's own width. I developed normalization methods and an exponential model that predicts bead width with about 90% accuracy, and that can be refit for other processes and materials.

First-author manuscript under advisor review.

Sharing the research

I've presented the work, titled Pointless: The Curves and the Beads Talk, in two poster sessions and two oral presentations at Wesleyan in 2026. My oral presentation at the Summer Symposium placed third for the Spark Prize for research communication.

Part II · Duke University · 2026

Autonomous Robotic Laboratory

Helping build an autonomous materials lab, where AI agents run experiments with an industrial robot arm and a Hyrel 3D system for chemical vapor deposition (CVD).

Lab
Wang Lab, Duke University
PI
Haozhe (Harry) Wang, Ph.D.
Role
Research intern (remote) · Nov 2026–present
Focus
Robot arm + Hyrel 3D system · CVD

01 — The system

Lab equipment that runs its own experiments.

I help integrate the lab's equipment, an industrial robot arm and a Hyrel 3D system used for chemical vapor deposition, with AI agents so experiments can be planned and carried out without a person at the bench.

The work builds directly on the industrial robot programming I do for my WAAM research at Wesleyan.
